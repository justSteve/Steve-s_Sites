What's MOO?

MOOs are text-based, virtual reality sites that allow people to connect to the same place at the same time. They are completely unlike conventional chat rooms you might be familiar with in that they allow the manipulation and interaction with cyber-objects in addition to live communication.

MOOs are widely used, public domain programs and are stable, mature environments. The biggest challenge facing a MOO user is lack of graphical information. VOUSI has partnered with a software developer to incorporate a state of the art HTML front end to our MOO.

In a MOO one uses commands to do many things:

- move between distict places
- manipulate objects
- interact with people who in reality may live thousands of miles away
- create new virtual places
- describe one's character, the places one creates and the objects one owns
- mail elect ronic messages
- or conduct classes - all electronically via the Internet or a private intranet

Why use a MOO for education?

To fully appreciate the implications of using a graphical front end it's necessary to understand how users interact in a conventional MOO. Recall that we've said people interact by transmitting text. Let's consider what a classroom exchange might look like:

[...and so on]

So long as the subject of study can be represented as text, the group can share information quite easily, and because they are 'talking' using text they are generating study notes at the same time. A critical, central tenet of our educational philosophy (and another example of how our use of technology reinforces and facilitates that philosophy) is that real-time, written communication forces one to stretch cognitive muscles and refine language skills.

Multi-media Capabilities Increase the Depth of Interaction.

Understanding the power of text-based interactions (and remaining committed to its primacy) we can safely and productively introduce the use of graphics and audio. A MOO capable of transmitting graphics and audio means our group above can extend their conversation to include photographs, charts, audio recordings, and hyper-links.

When learners incorporate hypertext as an alternative mode of communication they literally extend the language they use. The implications are as wide ranging as they are unknowable. One thing is certain: This model enhances the educational process. While we do not claim that it completely replaces conventional educational models, we are convinced it offers viable alternatives to a great many 'non-traditional' students and an exciting supplemental modality for all.
