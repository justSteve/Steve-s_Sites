What's a Virtual Educational Environment (VEE)?

The Athena University electronic campus is built on a system which we call a Virtual Education Environment (VEE). This system is designed around goals rather than tools, according to our technological philosophy. There are three main tools which currently make up the VEE that is Athena University's campus.

The MOO (Multi-user Object Oriented) system enables us to have meetings, discussions and other 'real-time' interactions in a virtual environment which is primarily text but which can also incorporate images, sound and other media from the web.
The World Wide Web enables us to publish and access documents and multimedia materials - text, images, sound, video, applications and more. The web gives us access to a wide variety of information including academic papers, art, music, and news. We can also use the web to provide access to curriculum, schedules, administrative information, student assignments, and other internal materials.
E-mail also enables us to exchange a wide variety of information. It is best used as a form of correspondence, for when you need to exchange information that can't wait to be viewed on a web page. For instance, if you wanted to send a note to someone asking them to meet you on the MOO at a particular time, e-mail would be the way to do it. And if an instructor wants their class to review some materials on the web before the next meeting on the MOO, they'd use e-mail to notify the whole class and give them the address (URL) for the materials.

To these three tools we can add anything that meets certain criteria of availability, functionality and effectiveness. These criteria are detailed in our technological philosophy.
