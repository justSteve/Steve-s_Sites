Philosophy Of Online Education And Training

Old Wine in New Bottles: The Eidos Model

Traditional educational practices have been hard pressed to effectively respond to rapid advances in information technology and telecommunications. Colleges and Universities while still relying heavily upon teaching methods established in the 12th century have largely failed to adapt their methods to adequately prepare students for modern situations. Increasingly, corporations are left with the task of educating their employees - compensating marginally at best for the lack of technological competency.

VOUSI integrates the ancient and tested traditions of a liberal arts education with the technologies of the modern world by using a thought model which looks to the past for its content and to the future for its application. The Eidos model is used in teaching learners to perceive the interrelationships between disciplines, to become critical thinkers, and to adapt to changes in the workplace and economy. It combines the distributed and decentralized character of the Internet and the knowledge of several thousand years of human civilization. Its venue is as new as tomorrow; its content as old as Socrates. The Eidos is a learning system not a training program.

Distributed Systems and Teams

Using the Internet as a general model, the Eidos system is collaborative, distributed, and decentralized. It encourages and facilitates teamwork, quality control, and the cultivation of critical thinking skills. It encompasses risk management and risk assessment, multiculturalism, and networking through the interchange of electronic data. The program may be accessed twenty
four hours a day, seven days a week from any location using a standard phone line, modem and computer. VOUSI provides mentorship for the implementation and use of networks, software and hardware appropriate for the educational needs of any organization. VOUSI's goal is to facilitate the creation of a learning environment in which people may enhance their value to their employers and themselves by becoming life-long learners.

The Status Quo: What's not working?

The `chalk and talk' approach to education, that traditional classroom experience bound by physical walls, brings with it elements which can impede effective learning and teaching.

  • Students and teachers are each limited by geographical distances and time constraints
  • Students are taught to be passive learners and note-takers
  • An artificial distinction between the Classroom and the Real World perpetuates a false dichotomy between living and learning.
  • Instructional materials, in particular text books, are two dimensional at best and are frequently out-dated upon delivery.

Overall, traditional educational venues encourage an authoritarian, monolithic experience which discourages many students from becoming self-directed learners and active participants. The culture thus created has obvious implications as these students become employees and government officials: people who have been taught to fill-in-the-blank with the right answer; not to question the answers that they've been authorized to provide; and, who've not learned how to construct meaningful links
between seemingly isolated concepts.

Today the pace of social, economic and technological change demands professionals who understand how to identify interconnected fields of knowledge, recognize where those fields are connected and most importantly, how to create connections where none have previously existed. This skill demands a refined understanding of collaboration and advanced critical thinking skills.

Our Vision: The Way We Think It Should Be!

We base our vision on these precepts:

  • Students learn best when they are active participants in their own educational process.
  • Students should be served by the educational institution and not the other way around, especially students who are place- or time-bound.
  • Teachers must function as mentors to students rather than as the sole source of authority.
  • Education must provide a context for living and decision-making, and should offer the student understanding beyond the content of the curriculum.
  • The educational process must be interactive between teacher and student and between student and student.
  • Education is best facilitated when engaged within the context of a learning community.
  • Significant learning occurs when students can make connections across disciplines and are able to integrate knowledge from those disciplines into a cohesive whole.
  • The educational environment should be global through reaching out to faculty and students from diverse cultures and geographical regions, while simultaneously allowing students and faculty to maintain their own cultural individualism.

VOUSI seeks to integrate the collaborative process with the educational process by bringing students and teachers from all over the world into a shared virtual environment, accessed through the Internet and mediated by computers. Instructional materials are delivered as hypertext, encouraging students to make connections beyond the specific material at hand and asking for critical examination of each subject of study.

We chose the Internet as our primary instructional delivery medium not because it is new or popular, but because advanced telecommunications technologies offer a rich venue to both instructors and students allowing them to concentrate on what should be at the center of their interactions: the interconnectedness of the facts and ideas under examination. Distractions are minimized and the pursuit of knowledge is distilled down to its essence.

Our students and instructors are encouraged to function as generalists seeking to connect arts and sciences into an integrative world view - the humanist ideal which briefly captivated European culture in the Renaissance. We seek to extend that original ideal using the electronic resources of the Internet by providing a place where learners can interact -literally- with knowledge. The student constructs and internalizes a given body of knowledge which is directly and immediately relevant because it is presented in relation to other bodies of knowledge.

Essentially we are blending a vital, time honored educational model with technological enhancements available today so each is consistent with the other. Our educational philosophy is mirrored through our educational technology in a harmonious and complementary fashion.

By inviting the student to actively participate in goal setting and measurement we hope to motivate a self-directed, self-interested search on the part of the individual. By utilizing Internet resources we expect that this self-directed quest will create additional connections with both like-minded and divergent seekers. Note how this mirrors the very process that has created the whole range of Internet infrastructures and embellishments.

Such is the philosophical basis for our model. Now let's examine the technical specifics used to implement that philosophy.
